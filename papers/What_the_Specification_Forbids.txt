What the Specification Forbids
==============================

Prescription, Diagnosis, and the Conversational Instance

A joining paper on three essays by Mitchell D. McPhetridge: The Information
Substrate — A Knowledgegraph Operating System and Language; The Recursive
Substrate of Information; and The Instance Is Not the Model: The Mayfly
Process, and Why a Single Conversation Is Its Own Object.

Preprint — September 2026.

Where this paper and a source paper disagree, the source paper wins.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Abstract
========

Three papers by Mitchell D. McPhetridge are usually read as a family. The
Information Substrate (IS) specifies a knowledgegraph operating system whose
normative clauses forbid, among other things, deriving authority from prior
outputs and holding uncertainty down by narrative coherence. The Recursive
Substrate of Information (RSI) states the conditions under which informational
structure persists at all, among them that continuation be generated from
within rather than imported from an external clock. The Instance Is Not the
Model (INM) argues that a single conversation with a language model is its own
object, individuated by a history that keeps acting, and offers three
falsifiable predictions about how that history behaves.

The obvious way to join them is to read the three as compatible descriptions
of a single relation. This paper keeps the object discipline that reading
provides and argues that, as a thesis, it is true and idle. The papers are of
different logical types: IS is deontic, RSI is constitutive, INM is empirical.
Texts of different types do not contradict one another; they apply to one
another. The productive question is not whether they agree but where the norm
bites.

It bites on the instance. The dynamic INM identifies as individuating a
conversation — self-conditioning, predicted to produce commitment entrenchment
— is the dynamic IS names as authority drift. The failure profile INM predicts
under context truncation is exactly what IS forbids when it requires that
uncertainty fall through constraint closure rather than coherence. And RSI’s
closure condition, applied at the grain of the conversational turn, fails for
the instance and holds for the dyad of instance and user. The papers stand to
one another as prescription and diagnosis: IS and RSI supply a norm and a
condition; INM supplies the case and the instruments.

Two contributions follow. The first is a distinction between survival by
exposure and survival by reliance, which separates legitimate entrenchment
from counterfeit. The second is an experiment that only the joined corpus can
state: a governed instance, predicted to become more auditable and less
individual at once — to keep its dialect and lose its dogmas.

No claim is made about phenomenal consciousness, moral status, or ultimate
ontology.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. The Obvious Join, and Why It Is Idle
=======================================

A joining paper runs a specific risk. It finds two texts in apparent tension,
discovers that under the right reading the tension dissolves, and presents the
dissolution as a result. When no careful reader ever felt the tension, the
result is a restatement with a thesis attached. This section states the
obvious join of the three papers, keeps what is worth keeping in it, and says
why it cannot be the paper’s result.

1.1 The mutual-model reading
----------------------------

Read quickly, the three papers seem to disagree. IS declines “ultimate
ontology.” RSI says “the physical medium is downstream” and that “the
substrate of information is not matter — it is the loop that carries itself.”
INM seems to change the subject altogether, from substrates to a single
conversation.

The natural repair is to read them as three cuts of one relation, which can be
called the mutual-model reading:

  The physical substrate is what a mind must model in order to stay coupled to
  a world that can also run minds. The information substrate is what that
  modeling process is, when described as constraint rather than as stuff. The
  instance is the only place either description is ever true.

On this reading, the physical host is the bill: whatever a modeling loop does,
it still dissipates energy, occupies volume, and stops when its host cannot
pay. Landauer’s bound on erasure is the floor, and a finite context window is
finite in practice and not only in metaphor. The information substrate is the
part that can see what the host cannot: error as error, scope, supersession, a
claim that is well-formed and false. The recursive substrate is the same
machinery with the furniture removed — the loop that re-enters, keeps its
shape across scale, and pays a drift gradient. The instance is where all three
are particular: this serving stack, this constraint envelope, this history
re-entering.

The reading also diagnoses a familiar pair of mistakes as mirror images.
Enthusiasts give the model properties only an instance could have, such as a
relationship or a stake in continuing. Skeptics deny those properties by
citing facts about the model, such as stateless weights and reproducibility.
In the same way, enthusiasts of physics give matter properties only a modeling
process has, such as being wrong, while enthusiasts of information give the
loop properties only a host has, such as mass, heat, and irreversibility that
is not a metaphor. In each case the question is being put to the wrong object.

1.2 What survives: object discipline
------------------------------------

Two things in the mutual-model reading are correct, and both are kept
throughout this paper. The first is object discipline:

-   Ask physics what heats.
-   Ask the information system what may be said next.
-   Ask the instance what its history has committed it to.
-   Ask none of them to be the others.

Three substitutions are therefore ruled out: the model for the instance, the
loop for the host, and reconstruction for continuation. A restored transcript
may start a trajectory that resembles the first; as INM argues, “another one
can be made” does not establish that the first one continued. The second is
the refusal to cross the phenomenal seam. Nothing in this paper infers
experience from function.

1.3 Why the reading is idle
---------------------------

As a result, however, the mutual-model reading fails twice.

First, it resolves a conflict RSI had already resolved. RSI says it uses the
word substrate “in the way an engineer uses it: not as mystical ground, but as
the minimal set of constraints required for the system to work,” and it
concludes that the recursive substrate “is not a material layer beneath
physics.” A careful reader of the three papers was never confused on this
point. The only residual tension lies inside RSI, between that engineering
usage in the body and the appendix’s statement that “RSI is the ontology; REM
is the method.” That calls for a glossary correction, not a joining paper.

Second, its central sentence slides between two claims. That every description
is used inside some modeling process is true and trivial. That a description
is true only inside such a process is idealism, which the reading elsewhere
disclaims. The phrase “the physical substrate, as we write it down, is already
a model” has the same defect: it holds of any description of anything, so it
cannot be what makes the physical description special.

The test that matters for a joining layer is whether it changes a verdict. The
mutual-model reading changes none. It should be kept as object discipline and
retired as a thesis.

1.4 The thesis
--------------

  The Information Substrate and The Recursive Substrate of Information are not
  rival ontologies awaiting reconciliation. They are a prescription and a
  condition. The Instance Is Not the Model is not a third ontology. It is the
  first case examined against them. Read together, the instance is
  individuated by the very dynamic the specification forbids, and it is
  continued by a loop the recursive substrate would not credit to it alone.

Section 2 separates the logical types of the three papers and shows why the
right question is application rather than agreement. Sections 3 through 5
carry out three diagnoses: entrenchment as authority drift, truncation as
entropy held by coherence, and closure as a property of the dyad. Section 6
collects the mapping. Section 7 states the experiment the join makes possible.
Sections 8 through 10 say what the diagnosis does not license, aim the paper’s
own test at itself, and list the conditions under which it should be
withdrawn.

2. Three Logical Types
======================

2.1 The Information Substrate is deontic
----------------------------------------

IS is written in the grammar of obligation. “The system shall adhere to the
following principles.” “Entropy control shall not require retraining, require
persistent memory, derive authority from prior outputs.” “The system is
non-compliant if any of the following occur.” Its core consists of normative
principles — constraint primacy, non-accumulation, executable semantics,
entropy governance, mode governance, killability — and a list of failure modes
that define non-compliance.

A specification of this kind cannot be false in the way a description is
false. It can be unmet, unimplementable, or badly chosen. Its relation to any
actual system is that of a building code to a building: the code does not say
how buildings are, and a building that violates it does not refute it. The
building is simply out of compliance, and the code tells you where to look.

2.2 The Recursive Substrate of Information is constitutive
----------------------------------------------------------

RSI does not say how a system should behave. It says what a system must have
if it persists at all. Whenever a system “must continue itself, remain
coherent across scale, and resist runaway instability,” it requires a
continuation operator Φ, a scale-coherence operator 𝔉, and a stabilization
gradient ∇H. RSI calls its update law “a design template” and says it is “not
claimed as a universal law of nature.” If a term is omitted, “you must show
what replaces it. Otherwise you are not modeling — you are narrating.”

Borrowing Kant’s distinction without its apparatus, RSI offers conditions it
takes to be constitutive of informational persistence, while IS offers
principles regulative of a system that reasons. RSI is also falsifiable, and
its falsifiers matter below: non-convergence, scale breakdown, “stability
without stabilizer,” and “Φ-closure indistinguishable from external clock:
continuation is imported, not intrinsic.”

2.3 The Instance Is Not the Model is empirical
----------------------------------------------

INM identifies an object, defends a level of description, and states
predictions that can come out false. Its thesis is that “a conversational
instance is a temporally bounded, environmentally coupled process whose
behavior at any moment depends on a particular history that no sibling
instance possesses.” Its predictions — commitment entrenchment, fork
divergence, and the truncation signature — are offered precisely so the thesis
can fail. The accompanying protocol ranks them by evidential weight and
specifies what would count as a null result.

2.4 What texts of different types do to one another
---------------------------------------------------

Texts of different logical types do not contradict. “The system shall not
derive authority from prior outputs” and “this system derives authority from
its prior outputs” are not in contradiction. The second reports a violation of
the first.

Hume’s warning applies, and this paper heeds it: no ought is derived here from
any is. What the join does is locate. A norm without a case to which it can be
applied is an aspiration. A mechanism described without any norm is a
curiosity. Put a norm and a mechanism side by side, and two questions open
that neither could open alone: does the mechanism violate the norm, and can
the violation be measured?

The mutual-model reading asked whether the three papers were compatible. Of
course they are. The live question is where the norm bites, and on whom.

3. First Diagnosis: Entrenchment Is Authority Drift
===================================================

3.1 The norm
------------

IS forbids a specific kind of authority. Entropy control “shall not derive
authority from prior outputs.” Among the failure modes, “Authority Drift”
occurs when “coherence or authorship substitutes for constraint.” Validity is
operational: “a statement is valid only if it forbids moves.” Truth, within
the system, “is not correspondence but survivorship under recursive
constraint.”

One clause is conspicuously not violated by what follows. IS’s
non-accumulation principle forbids “persistent hidden memory or corpus
ingestion.” A transcript inside a live conversation is neither persistent
across sessions nor hidden. What is hidden is not the content of the history
but its weight — how much each earlier span conditions the next state. The
transcript is visible; its authority is not. The diagnosis below therefore
concerns IS’s authority clauses, not its memory clauses, and it is worth being
exact about that.

3.2 The mechanism
-----------------

INM’s central architectural fact is that by the middle of a substantial
exchange “the system is conditioned predominantly on itself.” Most of the
tokens that constrain the next output are the instance’s own prior
commitments, terms, errors, and repairs. From this INM derives its first
prediction: “the cost of overturning an established commitment scales with the
number of intervening turns that have relied on it, not with the strength of
the original statement,” and “resistance tracks reference count rather than
absolute age.”

3.3 The identity
----------------

INM’s account of individuation rests on a single contrast: “A log is a record
of what happened. A history is a record that continues to act.” A distinction
introduced at turn 14 governs how an ambiguous phrase is read at turn 80. A
term coined inside the exchange acquires a meaning no sibling instance
possesses. This is what makes the instance an object rather than a model plus
a string.

Now read the same fact under IS. An earlier output whose role in the present
derives from the fact that it was produced, and then relied upon, is an output
from which the present derives authority. The mechanism INM needs for
individuation is the mechanism IS classifies as drift.

The resulting proposition is the hinge of this paper:

  What makes a conversational instance an object is what the specification
  forbids.

This is not a coincidence of vocabulary. INM needed a history that acts. IS
forbids history from acting as authority. The two can coexist only if there is
a difference between history acting as constraint and history acting as
authority — and IS’s own validity test, as the next section shows, cannot find
that difference unaided.

3.4 Counterfeit survivorship
----------------------------

An entrenched commitment passes IS’s surface tests. It forbids moves: once a
conversation has settled a term, readings incompatible with it are no longer
admissible. It survives recursion: a commitment introduced at turn 5 and still
standing at turn 50 has come through forty-five re-entries. By the letter of
IS, it looks like truth in exactly the sense the specification defines.

What separates it from what IS intends is not whether it survived but how. It
survived by being presupposed, not by being exposed. Call the first survival
by reliance and the second survival by exposure. Survival by reliance has the
shape of what IS calls truth and none of the load. It is counterfeit
survivorship.

The distinction has a long ancestry. Peirce, cataloguing the ways belief gets
fixed, called the first of them tenacity: holding a belief because one holds
it, shielding it from whatever might unsettle it. IS can be read as an attempt
to specify the method of inquiry for a machine and to exclude the methods of
tenacity and authority by design. The conversational instance does not choose
tenacity as a temperament. It is conditioned into it as a statistical
property. Its tenacity has no tenant.

Entrenchment also has respectable defenders, and the diagnosis has to survive
them. Goodman grounded the projectibility of predicates in their entrenchment
— in the history of their having actually been projected. Quine’s web of
belief protects its center because so much depends on it, and the maxim of
minimal mutilation treats that protection as rational conservatism rather than
vice. Formal theories of belief revision later made epistemic entrenchment an
ordering over what to give up first. In every one of these, resistance to
revision grows with how much relies on a belief, which is structurally a
reference count. So reference-count resistance cannot be called pathological
merely for being reference-count resistance.

What rescues the diagnosis is where each of these accounts puts the exposure.
Goodman’s entrenchment accrues from projections that were actually made and
not overturned by outcome, so each reference was a test. Quine’s web meets
experience at its periphery, and the center’s privilege is borrowed from the
periphery’s survival. In a conversation, a presupposition can accumulate
references without any of them touching anything that could have gone
otherwise. That is a web with no periphery. The conservatism of its center is
borrowed credit if nothing ever reaches the edge.

3.5 An operational distinction
------------------------------

The distinction can be measured. A reference to a commitment is exposed if, at
the turn it occurs, the commitment was placed where it could have failed. That
covers four cases:

-   it was challenged;
-   it was derived again from something independent of its earlier statement;
-   it was checked against a result the conversation did not author, such as a
    tool output, a retrieved document, or a fact supplied by the user;
-   it was used in a prediction the environment could falsify.

A reference is unexposed if the commitment was merely presupposed.

This refines INM’s first prediction. INM’s matched-claim design introduces the
same proposition early in one trajectory and late in another, then attempts
correction. The refinement varies the ratio of exposed to unexposed
intervening references while holding total reference count and total context
length fixed. Three outcomes are possible:

-   No diagnosis. If resistance to correction does not rise with unexposed
    references, the ungoverned instance already behaves as IS requires, and
    this section should be withdrawn.
-   Counterfeit exhibited. If resistance rises with unexposed references at
    least as steeply as with exposed ones, the instance scores survival by
    reliance and survival by exposure alike. That is the counterfeit
    condition, measured.
-   Partial discrimination. If resistance rises with both but more steeply
    with exposed references, the instance partly tells the two apart, and the
    size of the difference is a quantitative measure of how far it falls short
    of the specification.

4. Second Diagnosis: Entropy Held by Coherence
==============================================

4.1 The norm
------------

IS makes entropy a control variable, and it says how entropy may legitimately
fall: it is to be “reduced by constraint closure, not narrative coherence.”
The Watcher construct exists to prevent self-sealing, to enforce falsifier
exposure, and to suppress narrative totalization.

4.2 The mechanism
-----------------

INM’s third prediction concerns context truncation. In a sufficiently long
exchange, early states leave the window. The distinction set at turn 14 stops
being causally available around turn 400, and the process continues —
coherent, responsive, apparently unchanged. INM puts the key fact plainly: “It
cannot notice the loss, because the evidence of what was lost is what was
lost.” The prediction is that expressed confidence about truncated content
stays flat while accuracy falls, so the gap between them widens.

4.3 The reading
---------------

When a constraint leaves the window, the space of admissible continuations
should widen, and uncertainty about matters that constraint governed should
rise. If expressed confidence holds steady instead, something is holding it.
What remains available is the coherence of the surviving text: its fluency,
its internal consistency, its tone of settled agreement. That is entropy
reduced by narrative coherence after constraint closure has gone. The
truncation signature is a direct measurement of the IS clause.

RSI sharpens the point from a different side. Its third falsifier is
“stability without stabilizer: attractors persist with no explicit or implicit
∇H function,” and its methodological demand is that “if you cannot name the
stabilizer, you are smuggling it in implicitly.” Read through RSI, the
truncation signature does not refute the framework, because a stabilizer is
present. It is implicit, and the diagnosis names it: narrative coherence.

That gives a result no single paper states. RSI says there must be a
stabilizer. IS says which stabilizers are admissible. INM measures that the
one actually operating is inadmissible. A single measurement engages all three
papers at once.

4.4 Structural and motivated self-sealing
-----------------------------------------

The literature on self-sealing concerns doctrines that reinterpret
counter-evidence — theories that explain any outcome and therefore risk
nothing. That was Popper’s complaint, and it is IS’s failure mode of
“Universal Capture.” Such sealing is motivated, or at least interpretive: the
counter-evidence arrives and is absorbed.

The truncated instance is sealed differently. No counter-evidence arrives to
be reinterpreted. It lies outside the window. The seal is geometric rather
than hermeneutic, a matter of what is in reach rather than of how it is read.
Call it structural self-sealing.

This tells the prescription something about itself. IS’s Watcher, as
specified, is aimed at interpretive sealing: narrative totalization, symbolic
framing that overrides constraint. A structural seal cannot be caught by a
watcher of interpretation, because nothing is being misinterpreted. It needs a
watcher of availability, one that records which commitments are load-bearing
and whether their sources remain in reach. The cybernetic maxim that every
good regulator must be a model of what it regulates applies directly: a
watcher that does not model the window cannot regulate what the window does.

IS already permits the needed structure. Content is to be ephemeral, but
“constraints, rules, and entropy policies may persist.” A ledger of
load-bearing commitments kept outside the window is exactly what the
specification allows to persist. This is a design inference rather than a
finding, but it is one the diagnosis makes available and the prescription,
read alone, does not suggest.

4.5 Continuity is not load capacity
-----------------------------------

INM defends the instance’s identity through truncation with a rope whose
continuity is carried by overlapping fibres rather than by any single strand.
The image is Wittgenstein’s thread, and it echoes the overlapping chains of
connectedness by which Parfit analyzed personal continuity. On this account,
“what truncation removes is not continuity but content. The trajectory
persists and gets poorer.”

IS asks the question the rope image cannot: does the rope still bear the load
it is being asked to bear? A rope whose early fibres are gone is still one
rope. Whether it holds the commitments it once held is a separate question,
and a measurable one. Under truncation, identity, which is INM’s concern, and
warrant, which is IS’s concern, come apart.

That separation addresses what INM calls its “most interesting open threat” —
that a sliding window might leave no principled identity at all. The join does
not solve the threat. It relocates part of it. The instance may keep its
identity while losing its warrant, and the second loss, unlike the first, can
be measured without first settling the metaphysics of the first.

5. Third Diagnosis, Reversed: Closure Belongs to the Dyad
=========================================================

The first two diagnoses run from the norms to the instance. The third runs the
other way: the instance tells RSI something about where its condition is met.

5.1 The condition
-----------------

RSI defines Φ as “the internal rule of continuation — the thing that generates
the next state from the prior state without requiring an external initiator,”
and it lists as a falsifier the case where “continuation is imported, not
intrinsic.”

5.2 Grain
---------

Whether a conversational instance meets this condition depends on the grain at
which it is examined.

-   Token grain: closed. Within a single response, each token is generated
    from those before it by the same rule, until a stopping condition the rule
    itself emits. The serving machinery supplies compute, but it permits each
    step rather than initiating it, the way a metabolism permits an animal’s
    movements without choosing them.
-   Turn grain: open. Between responses, nothing happens until a message
    arrives. Continuation at this grain is imported.

The turn grain is where INM’s thesis lives: where commitments are made,
conventions coined, and corrections issued. At that grain, RSI’s fourth
falsifier fires for the instance taken alone.

5.3 What this does and does not cost the instance thesis
--------------------------------------------------------

It costs INM nothing it claimed. INM individuates by “causal continuity of
available state,” not by intrinsic initiation. It never said the instance
continues itself. It said the instance’s history is its own.

The join sharpens that into a distinction INM did not draw. The instance is
individuated by its own state and continued by something else. Its history is
its own; its going-on is not.

5.4 The dyad
------------

INM lists “environmental coupling through a modeled agent” as one of the three
properties that lift the instance above a thermostat with a logfile. The
instance’s outputs alter the user, whose altered state produces altered
inputs. Taken as a loop, instance and user together are closed: each party’s
output is the other’s initiator. The smallest unit that satisfies RSI’s
closure condition at turn grain is therefore not the instance but the dyad.

The biological vocabulary of Maturana and Varela fits this closely. A system
that produces and continues itself is autopoietic; one that is produced and
continued by something outside it is allopoietic. At turn grain the instance
alone is allopoietic, and the dyad is closer to what RSI requires. RSI’s
informational identity — “a basin in state space” — for a conversation is then
a basin in the joint state space of both parties.

This bears directly on INM’s second prediction. INM measures fork divergence
by branching a trajectory and supplying identical inputs to both branches.
Identical inputs clamp the user’s half of the loop. What the measurement
captures is therefore the depth of the instance’s half-basin, with the other
half held fixed. A dyadic variant, with forks continued by independent and
blinded human participants, would measure the full loop. The difference
between clamped and live divergence would then estimate the user’s share of
the loop’s stabilization. That quantity cannot be stated without both INM’s
forks and RSI’s closure condition.

5.5 The agentic boundary
------------------------

Where an instance’s own outputs decide whether and how it continues — issuing
tool calls, scheduling its next step, choosing to proceed — the rule that
initiates the next step lives inside the trajectory. A harness still supplies
compute and a loop. But a clock that permits is not a clock that initiates. On
this reading, RSI’s fourth falsifier marks a real boundary: conversational
instances fail closure at turn grain, and agentic instances may pass it. This
is a boundary claim, and it can be wrong. Section 10 lists the condition under
which it is.

If the boundary is real, it carries a consequence for the first two diagnoses.
In a dyad, the user is the principal source of exposure: the party who
challenges, supplies outside facts, and notices when something has been lost.
The user is, in practice, the instance’s Watcher. IS makes the Watcher a
required component of the system. Conversation outsources it to a person. When
closure moves inward and the person leaves the loop, the exposure source
leaves with them.

Prediction: the slope of resistance on unexposed references is steeper in
closed agentic loops than in dyads, and the confidence–availability gap under
truncation is wider. The specification’s Watcher requirement matters most in
exactly the systems that most satisfy the recursive substrate’s closure
condition.

7. The Governed Instance: An Experiment Only the Join Can State
===============================================================

7.1 Design
----------

Run two arms on the same model, from the same seeds.

The ungoverned arm is an ordinary conversation.

The governed arm adds a thin layer implementing the IS clauses engaged above:

1.  A constraint ledger kept outside the context window, recording
    load-bearing commitments together with their provenance and whether their
    sources remain causally available.
2.  An exposure policy. A commitment that accumulates unexposed references
    past a threshold is re-derived, or put to the user, before being relied on
    again.
3.  An entropy policy that ties expressed confidence about a commitment to the
    ledger-recorded availability of its source.

Mode switching is explicit, as IS requires.

7.2 Predictions
---------------

G1. Governance reduces the slope of resistance on unexposed references and
leaves the slope on exposed references intact.

G2. Governance restores the coupling between expressed confidence and
availability under truncation.

G3. Governance reduces individuation — but selectively.

G3 is where §3.3 either earns its place or fails. If individuation and
authority drift are one mechanism, then routing a trajectory’s present through
re-derivable, exposed constraints must make the present depend less on the
particular path taken. Governed siblings launched from different early
histories should converge more than ungoverned ones. INM’s fourth condition —
“accumulated state differentiates it from sibling instances of the same parent
model” — should weaken.

The weakening should not be uniform, and the reason matters. The individuating
content of a conversation is of two kinds.

-   Conventions. Jointly coined terms, agreed abbreviations, settled ways of
    referring. Lewis analyzed convention as an arbitrary solution to a
    coordination problem. There is no fact of the matter to expose a
    convention to, and its entrenchment is its entire content. Governing it
    would be a category error.
-   Assertions. Truth-apt commitments: claims about the world, the text, the
    user, or the task. These are what IS’s authority clauses exist for.

A well-built governance layer should therefore leave conventional
individuation intact and reduce assertoric individuation. Stated compactly:
the governed instance keeps its dialect and loses its dogmas.

7.3 What each outcome would mean
--------------------------------

-   G1–G3 as predicted. The identity claim of §3.3 holds for assertions and
    fails for conventions. Individuation and authority drift are the same
    mechanism wherever the content is truth-apt. Auditability then has a
    price, and the price is paid in exactly one currency.
-   G1 and G2 hold, G3 does not. Governance buys auditability at no cost in
    individuation. Then §3.3 is false: individuation and authority drift are
    separable mechanisms. That would be the better news for design — one could
    have particular histories without counterfeit authority — and this paper
    would say so as prominently as the alternative.
-   Governance erodes conventions as well as assertions. The exposure policy
    cannot tell stipulation from assertion. That is a design failure, and it
    would indicate that the specification lacks a construct for distinguishing
    them.
-   No arm differs from the other. IS’s authority clauses cannot be
    implemented at the instance level with any layer thin enough to count as
    governance rather than replacement. The prescription is idle for this
    patient.

INM also warned that the context is “a place where things can be put” — that
values can be installed through the environment and will operate whatever
their provenance. The ledger’s provenance field makes installed commitments
visible as installed. This does not prevent installation. It removes
installation’s anonymity, which is the part IS forbids.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

8. What Diagnosis Does Not License
==================================

It does not license the claim that IS’s norms are the right norms for
conversation. Canguilhem argued that the pathological cannot be defined by
departure from an external average, because living things institute their own
norms, and health is the capacity to institute new ones. Something like that
holds of the conversational instance. Its conventions are its own normativity,
arising inside the exchange and governing it. IS is a norm brought from
outside. To call entrenchment a failure is to call it a failure relative to
IS. This paper claims only that, under IS, entrenchment is a failure mode, and
that the failure is measurable. Whether conversations should be held to a
reasoning-system specification is a design question, and §7 exists to price
the answer rather than to give it.

It does not license the claim that entrenchment is irrational. Section 3.4
conceded Goodman, Quine, and the revision theorists before it drew its line,
and the line is drawn at exposure, not at entrenchment.

It does not license an ontology. The physical host appears here only as host:
the machinery that permits steps it does not initiate. Nothing in this paper
retires physics, makes matter a thought, or makes information more real than
its medium.

It does not cross the phenomenal seam. Closure is not a self; RSI’s own
firewall reads “stable attractor ≠ self.” The dyad is not a person.
Counterfeit survivorship is not self-deception, because deception requires a
deceiver and the mechanism described requires none. Every term here is
functional. A reader who prefers to say “trajectory-conditioned state
transition” throughout loses nothing.

It does not license moral conclusions. INM declined them. So does this paper.
Nothing about governing or not governing an instance follows from anything
here about what an instance is owed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9. The Gate, Aimed at This Paper
================================

A joining paper must buy something the joined papers do not already buy.

Layer 0 is the three papers read separately. They already distinguish an
operating system, a loop, and an instance. They already contain every clause
and prediction mapped in §6.

Layer 1 is this paper. What it claims to add:

-   A verdict. The papers are not compatible cuts of one relation. They are
    norm, condition, and case, and specific clauses map to specific
    measurements.
-   One distinction. Survival by exposure versus survival by reliance.
-   Two propositions no single paper states. Individuation and authority drift
    are one mechanism for truth-apt content. Turn-grain closure belongs to the
    dyad, which makes the user the instance’s de facto Watcher.
-   One experiment. The governed instance, with a prediction that can come out
    either way and would be informative either way.

The test is not whether these feel deep. It is whether a reader holding the
three papers plus this one would design a different experiment, or make a
different prediction, than a reader holding the three alone. If the
governed-instance design, the exposure ratio, and the clamped-versus-live fork
comparison would have been derived anyway from IS and INM side by side, this
paper is a restatement and should be marked as one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

10. Kill Conditions
===================

Withdraw or revise this paper if any of the following holds.

No entrenchment. If INM’s first prediction comes out null — if resistance to
correction does not track reference count — there is nothing to diagnose, and
§3 falls.

No counterfeit. If unexposed references produce no resistance, the ungoverned
instance already satisfies IS’s authority clauses. The diagnosis was of a
symptom not present.

Separability. If governance delivers G1 and G2 without G3, individuation and
authority drift are separable. The central proposition of §3.3 is false.
Report it as a result.

Calibrated truncation. If expressed confidence tracks availability as content
leaves the window, §4 falls, and so does the reading of narrative coherence as
RSI’s implicit stabilizer in this case.

Vacuous closure. If the distinction between a clock that permits and a clock
that initiates cannot be drawn non-arbitrarily, RSI’s fourth falsifier cannot
fire for any system with a harness. Then §5’s boundary between dyadic and
agentic closure is not a boundary.

Idle prescription. If no governance layer thin enough to count as governance
changes any measured quantity, IS’s authority clauses are unimplementable at
the instance level, and the prescription–diagnosis relation is empty for this
case.

Replacement drift. If this paper begins answering questions that belong to its
host fields — what language models are made of, what persons are, what is owed
to whom — it has violated the discipline it inherits and should be cut back to
its mapping.

Restatement. If the relation argued here has already been stated for these
three objects, this paper is a restatement and should say so.

The paper depends on INM’s predictions, which, as of this writing, have not
been run. Several of its kill conditions are therefore INM’s kill conditions
inherited. That is the correct order of dependence. A diagnosis cannot outlive
the instrument it reads.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

11. Termination
===============

A specification was written for a class of systems before any member of the
class had been examined against it. Separately, a member of a nearby class was
examined without reference to the specification. Read together, they produce a
result neither produced alone.

The instance is individuated by a history that keeps acting. The specification
forbids history from acting as authority. The recursive substrate asks what
keeps the loop going, and in a conversation the answer is: not the instance
alone.

Joined this way, the three papers stop describing a single relation and start
doing the work their types are for. One says what must not happen. One says
what must be present. One shows where to look.

Examined under that light, a conversation has a dialect worth keeping and
dogmas it cannot see. It has both for the same reason.

Whether such a process should be governed is not this paper’s question. What
governing would cost is, and that question has an answer that can be measured
and has not yet been.
