A DUAL-MODE SORTER FOR MPLPB: TURNING UNFORMATTED MATERIAL INTO INSPECTABLE CANDIDATES
======================================================================================

Source-Anchored Candidate Extraction for Closed Local Ledgers

Mitchell D. McPhetridge

Independent Researcher

Technical report, version 1.0, 17 November 2026

Prepared with AI-assisted implementation and writing. The experiments
described here are developer-authored software controls, not independent
semantic review, peer review, or verification of historical truth.


Abstract
--------

Unformatted document collections contain useful identifiers, dates, repeated
wording and documentary references, but those observations do not establish
event identity or historical relationships. A preprocessing tool can make this
material inspectable without giving its interpretations the authority of the
original records. This report describes an implemented offline sorter for
Combined MPLPB, a closed local ledger whose reader requires eligible source
support under a finite answer grammar. The sorter preserves UTF-8 source
hashes and exact byte and character spans, assigns provisional labels, and
emits either textual observations alone or the same observations plus non-
authoritative comparison proposals. The two modes have identical node arrays;
comparison adds review edges rather than merging source occurrences. A native
export stores the graph in a sealed, retired MPLPB page after strict format
validation and exact graph round-trip checks. An unchanged pre-sorter checkout
validates and displays that page while withholding it under all three delivery
profiles. Eight fictional development files produce 176 identical nodes in
both modes, 264 observation edges and 77 additional comparison edges. The
frozen refined implementation passed 1,184 Python tests and 52 public-evidence
controls. These results establish tested software behavior and native format
compatibility. They do not measure historical relationship accuracy, answer-
completion gains, or general historical reasoning.

Keywords: source provenance; closed local ledgers; candidate extraction;
documentary occurrences; fail-closed retrieval; inspectable preprocessing.


1. Problem and contribution
---------------------------

A directory of notes, letters and extracted document text is not yet a
reliable knowledge graph. A subject line may recur across unrelated
correspondence. Several copies may describe one message. A repeated identifier
can accompany a changed body. A sentence containing a correction may be
quoted, negated, conditional, or limited to one claim. A printed date may
identify when a letter was written rather than when the described action
occurred.

The useful first step is therefore to organize what can be inspected,
preserving what remains unresolved. Combined MPLPB already supplies native
pages, source hashes, revisions, serving restrictions, provenance checks and
refusals [1, 2]. The sorter is an adapter beside that system, not a
replacement answer engine. Its implementation is in mplpb_work/sorter.py; its
tests and evaluation runner are retained with the repository [3, 4].

The contribution is the implemented combination of reversible textual
extraction, identical observations across two modes, explicitly provisional
comparison edges, and native retired-page packaging that the existing reader
can validate and withhold. The report makes no claim of world-first priority.
It does not claim to solve entity resolution, event co-reference, historical
truth or free-form question answering. Those problems require evidence and
evaluation beyond this adapter.


2. Authority boundaries
-----------------------

Six states must not be collapsed into one another:

- Document integrity: the bytes or sealed page match their recorded hash.
- Quotation fidelity: an excerpt matches an identified source span.
- Source-attributed statement support: eligible wording satisfies an
  authorized proof rule.
- Candidate historical interpretation: a proposed event, identity or
  relationship needs review.
- Human-reviewed interpretation: an actual reviewer has assessed the
  interpretation.
- Independently established historical truth: evidence outside this extraction
  process establishes the historical proposition.

The sorter operates at the first two levels and proposes items at the fourth.
It does not award the third, fifth or sixth. A successful native format check
establishes valid packaging, not semantic support. A matching identifier
establishes shared text, not shared event identity. The author and development
process are not independent reviewers of their own outputs.

Every graph assessment retains answer_supported: false,
source_support_established: false, and
historical_truth_independently_verified: false. Serving eligibility is
explicitly unevaluated. These flags apply to the graph, sources, nodes and
edges. The emitter rejects attempts to promote those assessments. Original
source headers are not permission to activate a page or bypass source
restrictions.


3. Input and source anchoring
-----------------------------

The input is a directory containing UTF-8 .txt, .text, .md or .markdown files.
PDF extraction, OCR and network acquisition are outside this tool. Literal
URLs may be recorded but are never fetched. The runtime uses the standard
library and the repository's bounded date-cue helper; no model or paid service
is required.

Files are decoded strictly without Unicode or newline normalization. Each
source has a raw SHA-256 hash. Each extracted span records its source
identity, source hash, relative path, half-open character coordinates and
half-open original-byte coordinates. Characters here are Python Unicode code
points, not display columns or grapheme clusters. The byte coordinates refer
to the original UTF-8 encoding. BOM, CRLF and multibyte-character tests
exercise that distinction [4].

A source identity incorporates its relative path and byte hash. Two files with
identical bytes remain distinct source appearances. Derived source occurrences
cover complete files or layout candidates separated by repeated explicit
identifier headers. Those boundaries are inspection aids, not proof that a
file contains exactly one historical document or event.

Paragraph statements retain their original text. Extracted features link to
those statements and source occurrences. Full occurrence spans refer back to
the file rather than duplicating all bytes inside every node. If a paragraph
exceeds the statement budget, it is skipped with a partial-output diagnostic
instead of being silently cut into an apparently complete assertion. Originals
remain unchanged.


4. Staged extraction and provisional labels
-------------------------------------------

The implementation separates extraction, status assignment, comparison and
graph emission. That separation permits inspection before any comparison
occurs [3]. Extraction identifies recognized identifier headers, sender and
recipient headers, subject lines, documentary date headers, identifier-like
tokens, explicit reference wording, literal URLs, correction cues and selected
action words. It also extracts repeated four-word lexical phrase keys. Their
normalization is recorded: casefolding and punctuation/whitespace differences
are ignored for the key, while the original span is retained.

The action-word vocabulary produces possible event candidates, not completed
events. Seeing "requested" or "reported" does not establish execution. The
implementation conservatively marks some quoted, conditional and negated
wording, but this finite recognition is incomplete. All results remain
candidates even when no qualifier was recognized.

A literal Status header can produce current_candidate or retired_candidate.
Conflicting declarations remain ambiguous. An absent declaration remains
undetermined. These are provisional annotations; they do not write MPLPB
status metadata into the originals. Source occurrences and action-word event
candidates remain different node kinds, with no established historical event
identity.

Date roles are likewise limited. Recognized headers can distinguish written,
sent, received, published or revised dates. A generic Date header remains a
source-stated date with unresolved role, and body dates are not silently
converted into event dates. There is no automatic chronology from a sorted
list of dates.


5. The dual-mode contract
-------------------------

Let N be the extracted nodes and E0 the containment and textual-co-occurrence
edges. With identical inputs, limits and optional prior assessment, the two
modes satisfy:

    not-compared: sources, N, E0
    compare:     sources, N, E0 followed by proposed review edges
    
    nodes(not-compared) == nodes(compare)
    sources(not-compared) == sources(compare)

The equality includes exact source spans and provisional statuses. Compare
does not rewrite nodes, resolve actors, promote statuses or introduce merged
nodes. The observation-edge array remains an identical prefix. Mode,
comparison flag, graph digest and possible comparison-budget diagnostics
differ in the envelope; the complete envelopes are not claimed to be byte-
identical.

Not-compared stops at observations. It can record a reference mention or
correction cue without resolving a target. Compare can add shared-header
reviews, repeated- phrase reviews, explicit-reference candidates, possible
corrections and candidate identity clusters. Cluster membership is a hyperedge
annotation over existing occurrences, not a new merged event node. Confidence
is null rather than an uncalibrated probability; evidence anchors and notes
state the proposal basis.

Occurrences sharing an explicit identifier may be clustered for review.
Changed body hashes are flagged. Identical subject wording alone does not
create an identity cluster, and repeated copies do not establish independent
corroboration. There is no transitive closure, unsupported chain completion or
inferred chronology.


6. References, corrections and missing evidence
-----------------------------------------------

Reference comparison is bounded and lexical. An explicit reference can produce
a candidate documentary edge only when the relevant declared identifier has a
unique, unqualified, non-self target within the inspected material. A missing
target stops the link. An ambiguous identifier stops it as well. A similar
subject or phrase cannot substitute for the missing record.

An unresolved-reference review edge points from the mention back to its own
source occurrence. It is an audit annotation, not a guessed documentary hop.
The reason distinguishes missing-or-outside-budget, ambiguous identifiers,
self/local identifiers and qualified or quoted wording. Missing inside a
bounded inspection is not proof of absence from the historical record.

Possible partial corrections retain their statement quotation, target
evidence, literal effective-window expressions and applied: false. An example
fixture says that only a requested count changes while the destination remains
unchanged. The graph preserves both statements. It neither updates the earlier
count as fact nor retires the original page. Negated, conditional and fenced
correction examples exercise stopped positive relations. Finite lexical guards
are not a complete semantic interpretation of every historical construction.

These review edges are intentionally weaker than operational MPLPB
corrections. An operational ledger write requires the existing explicit
workflow and authority. Read-only sort, compare, verify and peruse do not
rewrite ledger history.


7. Machine-readable output and retained assessments
---------------------------------------------------

The JSON schema is offline-candidate-sorter-v1. Its main arrays are sources,
nodes and edges. Nodes have stable derived identifiers, typed lexical
attributes, source spans and assessments. Edges carry endpoints, kind,
evidence-node IDs, assessment and notes. Compare-only edges are marked
comparison_only: true. Cluster edges additionally retain occurrence members,
identifier namespace, shared key and a changed-body flag.

The envelope records limits, exclusions, completeness, source bytes read, an
implementation hash and a canonical graph digest. complete: true means that
the implemented extraction finished within its stated bounds; it does not mean
that all relevant evidence was found or understood. A graph digest is not a
signature or an assertion that its interpretations are correct.

The optional prior graph is retained as a literal, hash-pinned JSON snapshot
with an untrusted-history label. It preserves earlier statements and
assessments without using them to establish current identity or support. Prior
history is bounded; an oversized history is refused rather than silently
discarded.

Verification reloads current source bytes and deterministically replays the
graph. It rejects source changes, inventory changes, forged redigested
proposals and implementation drift. This is source-fidelity replay, not an
independent semantic verifier. Changes to the implementation fingerprint
intentionally require a fresh extraction; prior graph artifacts remain
inspectable as historical outputs.


8. Native MPLPB packaging without reader changes
------------------------------------------------

JSON itself is not an eligible native answer page. The explicit export
operation writes an escaped graph into a machine-origin, unratified native
page, using the existing ledger writer. The page is marked external=no and
withdrawn in a private staging ledger before publication. The requested
destination must be new and outside the unchanged source tree.

The refined exporter re-reads staging with the native ledger, requires no
native validation findings, checks a single intact retired page, and checks
exact graph serialization and JSON recovery. The check includes the writer's
normal heading. It then verifies that no non-index record is servable before
publishing the new directory. Tests show that native validation errors,
warnings, wrong graph bodies and interrupted retirement do not publish the
requested candidate destination [6]. This is not a claim of crash-proof
storage or isolation against a hostile host.

The native acceptance and withholding distinction is explicit:

Existing interface         Meaning of observed result
-------------------------  ---------------------------------------------
validate --strict, exit 0  Native page format and integrity checks pass
show, exit 0               Retired page remains available for inspection
gate, exit 3               No eligible source bundle is available
ask, exit 3                No supported answer or claim is returned

The lab and internal gates exclude the snapshot as retired. External applies
the sealed external=no restriction before its retirement check. All three
therefore withhold the page. Historical as-of tests also remain unsupported.
Native show uses the reader's existing normalized display; exact whitespace is
inspected in the stored graph, original files and byte/character anchors.

A sealed graph snapshot must not be reactivated as historical evidence.
Unchanged originals can be imported separately through ordinary MPLPB source
workflows with their actual ownership, provenance and restrictions. The sorter
cannot infer or authorize those policies from a directory of raw text.


9. Bounded operation and partial output
---------------------------------------

Resource                  Default bound
------------------------  -----------------------------------------------
Files and bytes           64 files; 1 MiB per file; 8 MiB per source pass
Tree traversal            4,096 entries; directory depth 12
Graph size                4,096 nodes; 8,192 edges
Statements                2,000; 8,000 characters per statement
Comparisons               20,000 proposal attempts
Phrase extraction         4,096 phrase keys; 32 phrase groups
Prior history and output  1 MiB prior JSON; 32 MiB serialized graph

Limits may be lowered, not raised through the override interface. Hidden
entries, symlinks and nonregular files are excluded. Traversal, source reads
and output sizes are bounded, with explicit diagnostics for incomplete
processing. Two source passes verify that included source bytes and inventory
remain stable. Outputs cannot overwrite inputs or existing outputs and are
created privately.

Further finite proposal-selection rules apply: shared-subject pair reviews
consider at most the first 16 occurrences per subject. Completeness does not
mean all possible review pairs were enumerated. These are algorithmic and
byte-count bounds, not a hard operating-system wall clock deadline. Filesystem
checks assume a trusted local host. A cap can stop a relationship because the
needed record was outside budget. The correct result is a visible limitation,
not an inferred missing link.

The refined implementation is pinned to commit

1,184 Python tests in 353.340 seconds of reported test time. JavaScript API,
browser transport, crawler fixtures, actual pinned Pyodide WebAssembly and

software regressions, not 1,184 independent historical accuracy observations.

For native compatibility, an unchanged checkout of
3df2c6e22f0ef454f3ee67b88d43402a42f63443, before the sorter existed, was used
for validate, show, gate and ask. Its native reader packages are byte-
identical to the refined checkout. It validated and displayed the exported
page, and withheld it under lab, internal and external. The retained report
records per-file hashes and commands. A deliberate reader-package mutation is
refused by the compatibility runner before executing the comparison. This
demonstrates compatibility, not an independent implementation of the proof
kernel.

Tests also cover byte preservation, ambiguous identifiers, stopped
intermediate links, source corruption, forged graph data, unchanged-key body
mutations, prior history, resource exhaustion, offline URL handling and
prevention of candidate promotion. Every negative case keeps unsupported
conclusions unsupported.


11. Costs and failed attempts
-----------------------------

The retained native-export development run used Python 3.12.14 on a shared
Linux x86_64 environment with five visible CPUs. Hardware allocation was not
pinned. The source fixtures total only 1,402 bytes; these timings are not a
scaling study.

Operation              Wall seconds  Child CPU seconds
---------------------  ------------  -----------------
Not-compared sort      0.142         0.142
Compare sort           0.149         0.148
Native retired export  0.192         0.191

The corresponding cumulative child maximum RSS readings were 19,116,032;
19,542,016; and 24,961,024 bytes. These are process-lifetime child maxima, not
isolated incremental memory or aggregate process-tree memory. Each sort
reports 2,804 source bytes read across two passes. That metric excludes
implementation and ledger reads and subsequent export verification. Python
startup and filesystem work are included in the timing. No speedup is
established by these single runs.

A first round-trip assertion incorrectly omitted the native writer's standard
h1 heading and failed two export tests. The sorter expectation was corrected
to the existing native format; the writer and reader were not changed. The
failed log is retained. An earlier external-profile assertion expected
retirement to be the first exclusion, overlooking the existing external=no
precedence; the assertion was corrected. An earlier browser check initially
lacked its expected executable and was successfully retried with installed
system Chromium. These records show engineering iteration, not hidden
successes or independent validation.

The broader retained 22-question historical experiment was not rescored for
this sorter. No historical supported-answer gain is attributed to
preprocessing, more candidate edges, or additional passing software tests.

The completed native-export engineering record is commit
26ca2b5be17281684551ca2649ff62e7c0aa250f; the frozen sorter runtime is the

separate receipts.

From the completed engineering checkout, use fresh output paths:

    python -m mplpb_work.sorter sort tests/fixtures/sorter \
      --mode not-compared --output /tmp/sorter-raw-new.json
    python -m mplpb_work.sorter sort tests/fixtures/sorter \
      --mode compare --output /tmp/sorter-compare-new.json
    python -m mplpb_work.sorter verify /tmp/sorter-compare-new.json \
      --sources tests/fixtures/sorter
    python -m mplpb_work.sorter export-retired \
      /tmp/sorter-compare-new.json --sources tests/fixtures/sorter \
      --ledger /tmp/sorter-ledger-new
    python -m mplpb_answers validate /tmp/sorter-ledger-new --strict
    python -m mplpb_answers show /tmp/sorter-ledger-new SORTER-CANDIDATES

Repeat gate and ask separately with each profile:

    python -m mplpb_answers gate /tmp/sorter-ledger-new \
      'sorter candidate inspection' --profile lab --json
    python -m mplpb_answers ask /tmp/sorter-ledger-new \
      'What is Owner for record MAIL-001?' --profile lab --json

The gate and ask commands return exit 3; replace lab with internal or external
for the other profiles. Format validation and show return exit 0. The
consolidated runner retains every command and output:

    python -m tools.evaluate_sorter --output /tmp/sorter-run-new
    python -m unittest tests.test_sorter -q
    python -m tools.reproduce_boundaries \
      --revision 30f0d89c5954b1f1226d0e30c78dc671011849e1 \
      --output /tmp/sorter-archive-new --suite full

The guide supplies the separate-checkout --reader-checkout procedure [7].
Frozen source archives, implementation pins, command logs, package hashes and
failed attempts allow a third party to rerun the software checks. Expected
semantic answers are not supplied to the sorter. No blinded historical scoring

13. Limits and next experiments
-------------------------------

The sorter does not resolve arbitrary pronouns or ambiguous historical actors,
recover missing records, establish event co-reference, infer motives, or prove
causality. Lexical scope recognition can miss qualifications. A date
expression can remain unresolved. Matching prose can describe different
events, or several sources may repeat one unverified account. Candidate
display is not corroboration.

The native snapshot is deliberately withheld. That makes the conversion useful
for inspection and controlled later ingestion; it does not by itself make
source claims available for supported answering. Activating a candidate graph
as evidence would violate the intended boundary. Usability with large
authentic collections, full source-policy workflows and cross-platform
behavior needs additional testing.

Independent historical evaluation should freeze authentic sources, unseen
questions, review criteria and compute budgets before measuring relationship
precision, recall, false bridges, appropriate refusals and human effort.
Unknown quantities must stay null. A fresh AI assessment may help inspect
development outputs if labeled as AI assessment; it is not automatically
independent human validation or truth authority. The current contribution is
narrower: an inspectable adapter with tested source fidelity and native
withholding, preserving the evidence contract for later work.


14. Conclusion
--------------

The implemented dual-mode sorter turns raw text into reversible observations
and optional review proposals while keeping the existing MPLPB reader in
control of eligibility and support. Its strongest demonstrated property is the
separation of inspection from assertion: identical nodes across modes,
explicit non-authoritative edges, stopped missing links, and a validated
retired native snapshot that remains withheld. The retained tests make those
boundaries reproducible. Historical reconstruction and historical truth remain
separate, unproven objectives.
